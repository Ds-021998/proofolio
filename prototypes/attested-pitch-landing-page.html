The criteria you were marked on, back when you need them.

Every time a marker judges a piece of work against a criterion, they make a judgement. And the moment the mark is released, that judgement is thrown away. Attested keeps it.

Why now

AI made explaining feedback abundant and free. It made attested evidence of ability scarce.

Any model will explain a rubric to a student at two in the morning. None of them can vouch for what that student can actually do. We don't compete on explanation — we keep the thing a model cannot produce: a dated judgement, against a named capability, made by someone the institution stands behind.

The mechanic

One criterion, used four times instead of once.

We don't add an artefact. The coordinator already writes these — we stop throwing them away after marking.

Who it's for

Three roles, and nobody does extra work.

The student

Sees only their own

The criteria before writing, the ones still open carried into the next submission, and feedback compared against the last time the same criterion was judged — closed, still open, or newly open.

They can point a marker at where they addressed something. They cannot mark it done. Whether it's fixed is the marker's call.

The sessional marker

Their group only

Judging instead of typing, and a tutorial brief counted from their own marking — the three things their group missed most, with de-identified sentences from the scripts.

Nobody wrote that brief. No timer runs while they mark; time accrues quietly and only they ever see the total.

The coordinator

Cohort, aggregated

They own the criteria. They see where the cohort is failing while there are still weeks of teaching left, and a handover built from what markers noticed on the page.

Indicators attach to the criterion, not the marker — which is what makes them survive seven of eight sessionals leaving.

Guardrails

What it will never do.

Written down before the first line of code, because the easiest version of this product is the one nobody should build.

  • × Produce text a student could submit as their own
  • × Rank students publicly, or run a leaderboard on assessment
  • × Put a label on a student's own submitted work
  • × Grade, re-grade, or predict a grade
  • × Let anything leave the course that a marker hasn't closed
