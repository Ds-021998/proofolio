Teaching team

Where this claim comes from. Two of the students we interviewed asked for a record of their skills unprompted, before we showed them anything like it. We have not interviewed an employer, so we are not claiming recruiters use these today — only that the people being assessed asked for it first.

The mechanic

One criterion, used four times instead of once.

We don't add an artefact. Your coordinator already writes these — we stop throwing them away after marking.

01

Before you write

You see the standard

Published before you write, on the submission page itself. Not buried in a notification you'd have to remember.

02

While marking

The marker clicks, doesn't type

Three buttons. The comment written once at setup attaches itself, edited only where your script differs.

03

When you ask

Your question arrives anchored

Attached to the criterion, so the reply is already drafted from the standard. Your script is never reopened.

04

Next assessment

It carries forward until it closes

Still open, so it meets you at your next submission. It closes only when a marker judges it met.

Who it's for

Three roles, and nobody does extra work.

Role 01

Students

Hover to see

The criteria before you write, the open ones carried into your next submission, and a capability record that shows where you started as well as where you finished.

Role 02

Tutors

Hover to see

A tutorial brief counted from your own marking — the three things your group missed most. Nobody wrote it. And no timer runs while you mark.

Role 03

Coordinators

Hover to see

Where the cohort is failing with weeks of teaching left, and a handover built from what markers noticed — attached to the criterion, not the marker.

Guardrails

What it will never do.

Written down before the first line of code, because the easiest version of this product is the one nobody should build.

× Produce text a student could submit as their own
× Rank students publicly, or run a leaderboard on assessment
× Put a label on a student's own submitted work
× Grade, re-grade, or predict a grade
× Let anything leave the course that a marker hasn't closed
