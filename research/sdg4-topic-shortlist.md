# COMP7110 — SDG 4 topic shortlist and narrowing plan

_Source: project doc `claude/sdg4-topic-shortlist.md`, 20 Aug 2026. Rendered version: `sdg4-topic-shortlist.html` in this folder._

## Course constraint that decides the topic

COMP7110 is a customer-discovery course, not a research-paper course. All four assessments are
Business Model Canvas iterations built on real interviews; AI-generated or fabricated interview
data is explicitly banned. Iteration 3 (13 Nov) requires a working prototype with individual
contribution assessed from repository logs.

Consequence: the selection criterion is **whose problem can we interview 15+ times, starting now**,
not which SDG 4 issue is most globally important. Global framings (literacy in low-income
countries, refugee schooling, rural connectivity) are unavailable — the people cannot be reached
from St Lucia.

Frame the topic as a **person**, not a theme.

## The filter (a "no" on #1 is fatal)

1. Can we book 15 interviews in the next two weeks?
2. Do these people already work around the problem today? (an existing hacky workaround = real pain)
3. Can four students ship working code by 13 November?
4. Does it need ethics approval or access to minors? (interview teachers, not school students)

### Sharper wedges (avoid the generic version of each)

1. **Tutors/TAs** — not "AI marks it" (crowded, fraught under UQ integrity rules). Instead:
   consistency of marking *between* tutors, or turning an assigned mark into feedback the student
   can act on before the next assessment.
2. **International postgrads** — not "find jobs". Instead: the specific gap between what a
   coursework degree certifies and what an AU entry-level employer asks for, or how visa status
   filters applications before a human reads them. Evidence: 2024 GOS — 56.1% FT employment for
   international postgrad coursework vs 88.1% domestic (32-point gap); median salary $70k vs $100k.
3. **Teachers** — pick one named admin task (reporting cycle, parent communication, differentiating
   a lesson across ability levels), not "teacher workload". Evidence: AITSL 2024 — median 50-hour
   week, 18–19% spend 10+ hrs/wk on admin, 36% intend to leave before retirement, 69–75% cite
   workload/stress.
4. **Neurodivergent students** — the paperwork and disclosure loop (Student Access Plan), not the
   pedagogy. Requires genuine care in recruitment.
5. **Career switchers** — only viable if you serve ONE side; any marketplace has a cold-start
   problem unsolvable by November.

## Week-one narrowing exercise (decide by evidence, not by argument)

1. Each teammate claims a *different* candidate — nobody defends the one they already liked.
2. Book two 30-minute interviews each, by Friday. Failure to get a reply IS the finding.
3. Ask only about the past. Record with permission; keep transcripts for the canvas and reflections.
4. Fill the matrix together on Monday from what was actually heard.
5. Write the problem statement: "[Specific person] struggles to [specific task] because
   [constraint], and today they cope by [actual workaround]." Can't fill the last blank from a real
   quote → not a problem yet.
6. Commit and stop looking. Topic churn past week four sinks these projects.

## Interview question bank

**Ask:** "Walk me through the last time that happened." / "What did you do about it?" / "How long
did that take last week?" / "What else have you tried, and why did you stop?" / "Who else does this
affect?" / "What happens if you just don't do it?"

**Never ask:** "Would you use an app that…" / "Do you think X is a problem?" / "How much would you
pay for…" / "Wouldn't it be great if…"

Principle: people are unreliable about the future and reliable about the past.
