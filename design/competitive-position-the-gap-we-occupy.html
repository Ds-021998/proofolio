We are not replacing any of these

Three of the four incumbents are already deployed at UQ, and one of them is mandatory. A team of four students does not out-build Blackboard by November, and should not try. The question worth answering is narrower: with all four of these running, what still falls on the floor?

Gradescope makes the ten minutes of marking efficient. Nothing here makes those ten minutes count twice — once for the student before their next submission, once for you before your next cohort.

That second use is the product. Everything below is the evidence for it.

What each tool is for, and where it stops

Judged only against one job: turning a marker's judgement into a change in what a student does next. Every tool below is good at the job it was built for.

Tool Its actual job What it does well Where it stops
Learn.UQBlackboard Ultra · mandatory System of record — brief, submission, marks, release. Course rubrics, inline marking, per-attempt feedback, the gradebook everything else reconciles to. Feedback is filed per assignment and never resurfaces. A criterion failed in Assignment 1 is invisible while the student writes Assignment 2. UQ's own staff guide notes a Course rubric is not compatible with Turnitin — so even the criteria are split across tools.
Gradescopedeployed at UQ · nearest rival Marking speed and consistency inside one assignment. Reusable rubric items applied across submissions; edit an item mid-marking and it applies retroactively; students see which items were applied; regrade requests; rubrics import across assignments; per-item lookup of which scripts received it. All of it lives inside one assignment. Nothing carries a student's failed criterion into the next one, nothing pushes the standard to the student before they write, and the reason an item keeps failing never becomes anything the next tutor can read.
Turnitin Feedback Studiodeployed at UQ Similarity checking, plus faster comment writing. QuickMarks reusable comment library, inline annotation, attached rubric. QuickMarks are the marker's library, not the student's checklist. Same one-assignment silo, and a second rubric system that does not talk to the first.
Ed Discussiondeployed at UQ Course-wide Q&A, asked once and answered once. Fast threaded questions, anonymous posting, endorsed staff answers, searchable within the course, genuinely reduces duplicate questions. A question there is not attached to your feedback on this student's script, so the tutor rebuilds the context every time. And a student will not post "I don't understand the comment on my own paper" in front of the cohort.
Teams channelthe real incumbent Where markers actually say what they noticed. Zero friction, instant, no training, already open on everyone's screen. Ephemeral, invisible to the coordinator, never reaches a single student, and deleted in effect the day the contracts end. This is the one we are really competing with.

What we are actually pitching

One object, used six times. Not six features.

01 · TUTOR, ONCE

The checkpoint

Written once from the brief and rubric, before the assignment opens. Ten minutes, not ten minutes per script.

02 · STUDENT, BEFORE

The standard, published

The same checkpoints are what the student works against while writing — with last assignment's failures already flagged.

03 · TUTOR, MARKING

Three taps, not a paragraph

Met / partly / not met. The comment the tutor wrote once at setup attaches automatically and is edited only where this script differs.

04 · STUDENT, AFTER

It stays open

An unmet checkpoint does not expire with the assignment. It reappears in the next one until the student closes it.

05 · BOTH

Questions with the context attached

A question arrives on a checkpoint and a paragraph, with a reply drafted from the standard. The tutor never reopens the script.

06 · COORDINATOR

Causes, and a handover

Which checkpoints the cohort is failing, while there is teaching time left — plus a note attached to the checkpoint, not to a tutor who leaves.

What we do not do

Naming this is part of the pitch. A tool that claims all seven jobs is not credible.

  • We do not mark. A human sets the checkpoints and a human judges every one of them.
  • We do not re-grade, predict a grade, or dispute a mark.
  • We do not replace the gradebook, host submissions, or check similarity.
  • We do not detect AI use.
  • We do not rewrite a student's sentences — the student side returns criteria and questions, never replacement text.

What would actually convince you

"I'm not convinced" is an offer of a test. Here is the one we would like to run, and the result that would tell us to stop.

THE ASK

One assignment, five checkpoints

You or one tutor spends ten minutes setting them. We do not touch Blackboard, the gradebook, or the marks.

MEASURE

Three numbers

Minutes per script against your current average. How many students open the carried-forward list before the next due date. Whether the cohort view changes one teaching decision.

KILLED IF

Setup costs more than it saves

If writing the checkpoints takes longer than the typing it removes across the cohort, there is no product here and we will say so in iteration 3.

The question we most need answered is not about features: who owns the checkpoints — the coordinator who will be here next year, or the sessional tutor who will not? We have not interviewed a coordinator about this, which makes you the most valuable interview available to us this semester.
