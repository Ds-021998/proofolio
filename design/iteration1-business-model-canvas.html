01

How to read this canvas

The Week 2 method marks canvas items as hypothesis, fact, or killed. We use four states rather than three, because our tutor-side evidence is our most decision-relevant material and also our thinnest — collapsing "two participants said this" into the same bucket as "nobody said this, we inferred it" would misrepresent what we know.

Accepted

≥3 participants across ≥2 independent researchers, consistent direction, no contradicting evidence held. The only claims stated as fact.

Emerging

1–2 participants, or 3+ from a single researcher. Real evidence, below threshold. Carried to iteration 2.

Assumption

No interview evidence. Literature, desk research or team reasoning only.

Refuted

Contradicted by evidence in our own set. Shown struck through.

Provenance is separate from strength. Following our synthesis's evidence-handling principle, interview evidence and literature evidence are kept apart. An item supported only by published literature is an assumption on this canvas, however strong the source — because no participant of ours has confirmed it.

Hypothesis register

Verdicts are judged against the criteria we wrote before the interviews. Two structural caveats apply to all of them, stated here rather than buried.

Caveat 1 — the test plan and the evidence point at different people. Four of six test cards examine tutors, markers or coordinators, three of them marked Critical. Our evidence is 28 students and 3 tutors from one researcher, with no coordinators.

Caveat 2 — our criteria are percentages; our synthesis is qualitative. With n = 3 tutors each person is worth 33 percentage points, so the 70% and 90% thresholds are unusable by construction. We report them honestly as not met rather than reinterpreting them after the fact.

Pre-registered test cards

CardCriterion as writtenVerdictEvidence
TC1
critical		 <50% of students can name a specific action Partially accepted
mechanism confirmed, blanket claim refuted		 Could name an action: TZ-P002/003/004/005, DS-P002, ZL-P002/003. Could not: TZ-P001, ZL-P004/005/006, PC-P003/004/007, PC-P012. Roughly even — threshold not met. The causal clause holds: general comments → no action. Action follows when feedback is specific and a related task is open.
TC2
critical		 ≥70% of tutors have no way of knowing Not met
rewrite, don't re-run		 Only PC-P009 is straightforwardly blind; PC-P010 built a tracker, PC-P012 has a coordinator process. 1 of 3 = 33%. The right claim is that no institutional mechanism exists, so those who need one build it privately.
TC3 ≥40% students recall repeats and ≥90% tutors lack visibility Inconclusive
denominator unknown		 Repeats reported by PC-P003/004/007, DS-P002 (~4 of 28). But not every participant was asked, so the true denominator isn't recorded. Framing also partly refuted: PC-P004 found repetition reassuring, PC-P008 had none.
TC4 ≥60% notice shared misconceptions, <30% act structurally Untested Nothing in the evidence set addresses noticing or acting on cohort-wide patterns. No coordinator interviewed. Card not run — reported, not dropped.
TC5
critical		 ≥50% describe an existing workaround or wish for visibility Accepted
criterion met		 PC-P010 (personal Excel tracking feedback history and improvement) and PC-P012 (coordinator spreadsheet + QC) = 2 of 3 = 67%. Provisional at n = 3, one researcher. Our strongest result.
TC6
critical		 ≥50% prioritise efficiency over improvement; distinguish attitude vs structure Leans refuted
resolves in TC5's favour		 PC-P010 built and maintained an unpaid tracker — behavioural evidence of motivation beyond efficiency. Workload and efficiency appear as constraints, not preferences. On current evidence the limiter is structural, not attitudinal.
TC6 was built to kill TC5, and didn't. We wrote a falsification test for our own most attractive hypothesis, ran it, and the evidence pointed the other way. That is the result we would most want a reader to notice.

Limitations

  • Small qualitative sample; no population-level claims are made or supported.
  • Student evidence is substantially stronger than tutor or institutional evidence; no lecturer or course coordinator has been interviewed.
  • Interview guides were not identical across researchers, and some topics were not explored in every interview — absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.
  • PC-P011's grade/feedback misattribution evidence is secondhand and remains an investigation lead.
  • DS-P004's marker-inconsistency finding rests on one participant and is not generalised.
  • AI-related concerns (AM-P003, AM-P004) remain an emerging finding on a limited base.
  • The literature synthesis has not yet been integrated into theme-level evidence.
  • The mechanism behind the feedback-to-application gap remains undetermined.
